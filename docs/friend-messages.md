# Messages for friends

Short, casual invites — text / DM / group chat. The whole point is they DON'T
sound like an ad. Pick one, tweak it to sound like you. Honesty ("it's new,
mostly empty") reads as charming from a friend and desperate from a brand, so
lean into it.

## To a gardener friend (text/DM)

> I made a thing — milkweed.garden. It's a little photo feed just for native
> gardens: you tag the plants, it flags the keystone ones for your region, and
> you can watch other people's lawn conversions go from dirt to prairie. It's
> brand new and mostly empty, which is exactly the problem — would you post your
> garden? Yours is the kind of thing I built it for.

## To a close friend, casual (text)

> ok I finally built the garden app I kept talking about 😅 milkweed.garden —
> take a look? no account needed to just browse. brutal honest feedback welcome,
> it's a baby.

## Group chat / broadcast

> Hi friends — I built a small free app for native-plant gardens: milkweed.garden 🦋
> You can browse without signing up. If you've got a yard, a balcony pot, or a
> lawn you're slowly murdering for a good cause, I'd love a post from you to get
> it started. No ads, no algorithm, I promise not to become a tech bro about it.

## To someone who'll ask "is it safe / what's the catch"

> No catch — it's free, no ads, and I'm not selling anything. Browsing needs no
> account; posting is just a magic link to your email (no password). Photos get
> their location data stripped automatically so your backyard can't be pinpointed,
> and everything's screened before it goes public. It's genuinely just a nicer
> place to keep garden photos than Instagram.

## One-liner (bio, story, footer)

> milkweed.garden — a quiet photo feed for native gardens. 🦋

## If they want to help beyond posting

> Honestly the most useful thing is just posting one garden and telling me what
> felt clunky. If you know other native-plant folks who'd get it, sending them
> the link is gold — that's the whole growth plan right now.
